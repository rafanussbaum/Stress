\section{The Gall Bladder Meridian}\label{meridian!gall bladder}

The gall bladder and the liver complete each other. In the liver, the bile acid is stored, which get from here to the colon and serve to cleave up the nutrients (especially the fats).
As mentioned, the action of the bile acid gets weakened by cholesterol in the gall bladder.
If the cholesterol levels are rising to a point that the amount of bile acid isn't enough anymore to keep the cholesterol in the dissolved state. 
That leads to crystallization and to the formation of gall stones\index{symptom!gall stones}, which can be extraordinarily painful.
